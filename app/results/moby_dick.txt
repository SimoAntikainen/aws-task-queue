This text is the opening paragraph of Herman Melville's novel "Moby-Dick." Here's a summary:

The narrator, who calls himself Ishmael, describes his decision to go to sea as a sailor. He explains that when he feels depressed, restless, or contemplative about death, he sees sailing as a way to clear his mind and improve his mood. Ishmael views going to sea as a healthier alternative to violence or suicide, comparing it to how the Roman statesman Cato chose to end his life. He concludes by suggesting that many people share his affinity for the ocean, even if they don't realize it.
